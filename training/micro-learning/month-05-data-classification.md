# Month 5: Data Classification

**Deliver:** December 2026 (first Monday)
**Time to read:** 2 minutes
**ISO Control:** A.5.12

---

## Headline: Not All Data Is Created Equal — Treat It Accordingly

Knowing what data you're handling and how sensitive it is determines how you protect it. A customer list belongs behind access controls; a lunch menu does not.

## Why It Matters

Data breaches are most damaging when sensitive data is left unprotected. Over-classifying everything leads to "classification fatigue" where labels are ignored. Under-classifying puts the business at risk. Getting classification right means protecting what matters without slowing down everyday work.

## What To Do

- **Know the four levels.** **Public** (marketing materials, public docs), **Internal** (team documents, internal emails), **Confidential** (customer data, contracts, financials), **Restricted** (secrets, credentials, PII, encryption keys).
- **Label as you create.** When you save a new document, ask: who should see this? Apply the right permissions and storage location from the start, not as an afterthought.
- **Don't mix levels.** Don't paste Restricted data into an Internal chat channel. Don't attach Confidential files to an email going outside the organisation. If you need to share up-protected data, use approved secure sharing methods.

## Quick Check

Look at the last document you created. Does its sensitivity match where you stored it?

## Report It

If you discover sensitive data stored in an unprotected location (e.g., customer PII in a publicly shared folder), report it immediately. Move it to a secure location if you can; if not, restrict access and alert the data owner.
