# Month 10: Data Disposal

**Deliver:** May 2027 (first Monday)
**Time to read:** 2 minutes
**ISO Controls:** A.7.14, A.8.10

---

## Headline: Deleting a File Doesn't Mean It's Gone

Secure disposal means ensuring data can never be recovered — not just hitting "delete" or dragging to the recycle bin.

## Why It Matters

Data that isn't properly disposed of can be recovered from old hard drives, recycled devices, discarded USB sticks, or even cloud storage accounts that were "closed" but not purged. Attackers and dumpster-divers actively look for discarded hardware. Several high-profile breaches originated from improperly wiped drives sold on eBay.

## What To Do

- **Shred physical documents containing sensitive data.** A cross-cut shredder is the minimum. Don't put documents with customer data, financials, or internal strategy in the regular recycling bin.
- **Follow IT procedures for device disposal.** Never throw away a laptop, phone, USB drive, or external hard drive. Return it to IT for secure wiping or destruction. The same goes for printers and copiers — many have internal hard drives that store copies of every document printed or scanned.
- **Delete cloud data properly.** Empty the trash/recycle bin in cloud storage. Revoke sharing links before deleting files. If you're leaving a shared folder or project, ensure your access is removed and you haven't left sensitive copies in your personal workspace.

## Quick Check

When was the last time you disposed of a physical document or device? Did you follow the secure procedure?

## Report It

If you discover old devices or media (laptops, hard drives, USB sticks) in storage areas, don't plug them in to "see what's on them." Report them to IT for proper handling.
