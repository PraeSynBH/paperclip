# Month 4: Social Engineering

**Deliver:** November 2026 (first Monday)
**Time to read:** 2 minutes
**ISO Control:** A.6.6

---

## Headline: Hackers Don't Break In — They Log In

Social engineering is the art of manipulating people into giving up information or access. It exploits human psychology, not technical vulnerabilities.

## Why It Matters

Phishing is one form of social engineering, but attackers also use phone calls (vishing), text messages (smishing), and in-person tactics. They pose as IT support, a new hire who "needs urgent access," or a vendor with a "time-sensitive" request. The attacker's goal is to make you act before you think.

## What To Do

- **Verify identity through a second channel.** If someone calls claiming to be from IT and asks for your password or to install software, hang up and call IT back on their known number. The same applies to email and chat.
- **Watch for urgency and pressure.** "Do this now or your account will be locked" is a red flag. Legitimate requests give you time to verify. If you feel rushed, stop and ask a colleague.
- **Don't be afraid to say no.** It's better to briefly delay a legitimate request than to facilitate a breach. Anyone who gets angry when you verify their identity is someone who needs to be verified the most.

## Quick Check

When was the last time someone asked you for information or access that made you pause? Did you verify?

## Report It

If someone pressures you for credentials, access, or sensitive information — even if you're "pretty sure" it was legitimate — report it. Attempted social engineering that fails is still a near-miss worth tracking.
