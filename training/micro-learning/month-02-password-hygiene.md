# Month 2: Password Hygiene

**Deliver:** September 2026 (first Monday)
**Time to read:** 2 minutes
**ISO Control:** A.5.17

---

## Headline: Passwords Are Like Toothbrushes — Don't Share Them, Change Them Regularly

Weak or reused passwords are a gift to attackers. One compromised password can unlock multiple systems.

## Why It Matters

Credential stuffing — where attackers try stolen username/password pairs across services — is automated and constant. If you reuse your work password on a personal site that gets breached, our systems are exposed. The average cost of a credential-related breach exceeds $4 million.

## What To Do

- **Use a password manager.** Let it generate and store unique, complex passwords for every service. You only need to remember one strong master password.
- **Make them long, not just complex.** A passphrase like "correct-horse-battery-staple" is stronger and easier to remember than "P@ssw0rd!". Aim for 14+ characters.
- **Never share passwords.** Not with your manager, not with IT, not with anyone. If someone needs access, use proper access delegation or shared accounts managed through a password manager.

## Quick Check

How many of your work accounts use the same password?

## Report It

If you suspect your password has been compromised, or if someone asks you to share your password, report it immediately. Change any potentially affected passwords right away.
