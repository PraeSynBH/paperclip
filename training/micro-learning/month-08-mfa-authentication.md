# Month 8: MFA & Authentication

**Deliver:** March 2027 (first Monday)
**Time to read:** 2 minutes
**ISO Control:** A.5.15

---

## Headline: One Password Is Not Enough — Turn On MFA Everywhere

Multi-factor authentication is the single most effective step you can take to protect your accounts. It stops 99% of automated credential attacks.

## Why It Matters

Even the strongest password can be stolen — via phishing, a data breach at another service, or malware. MFA adds a second layer: something you know (password) plus something you have (phone, security key) or something you are (fingerprint, face). An attacker who steals your password still can't get in without that second factor.

## What To Do

- **Enable MFA on every account that supports it.** Not just work accounts — your personal email, banking, and social media too. Breaches often start on personal accounts and pivot to work.
- **Use an authenticator app or security key, not SMS.** SMS-based MFA can be intercepted via SIM-swap attacks. Authenticator apps (Google Authenticator, Authy, Duo) and hardware keys (YubiKey) are far more secure.
- **Never approve an MFA prompt you didn't initiate.** If your phone buzzes with an approval request and you're not logging in, that's an attacker trying to use your stolen password. Deny it and report it immediately. This is "MFA fatigue" — attackers spam prompts hoping you'll eventually tap "approve" to make it stop.

## Quick Check

Count how many of your accounts have MFA enabled. Now count how many don't. Which number is bigger?

## Report It

If you receive an unexpected MFA prompt, deny it and report it. This is likely an attacker with your password. Change the password for that account immediately.
