# Month 12: Supply Chain Awareness

**Deliver:** July 2027 (first Monday)
**Time to read:** 2 minutes
**ISO Controls:** A.5.19, A.5.20, A.5.21, A.5.22

---

## Headline: Your Security Is Only as Strong as Your Vendors' Security

Attackers increasingly target the weakest link in the chain — and that's often a third-party vendor, contractor, or service provider with access to your systems.

## Why It Matters

The 2013 Target breach (40 million credit cards) started with an HVAC contractor's stolen credentials. The 2020 SolarWinds attack compromised 18,000 organisations through a single software update. Every vendor, contractor, and SaaS tool you use expands the attack surface. You are a defender of that perimeter.

## What To Do

- **Verify before granting access.** If a vendor or contractor requests access to systems, data, or facilities, verify their identity and authorisation before providing anything. Don't accept "so-and-so said it was okay" without confirmation.
- **Share only what's needed.** When working with external parties, share the minimum data and access required. Don't give a contractor access to the entire shared drive when they only need one folder. Don't CC external contacts on internal threads.
- **Watch for vendor impersonation.** Attackers pose as familiar vendors with "updated bank details" or "new invoice portal" links. Independently verify any change request from a vendor — especially payment or access changes — using a known contact method, not the one in the suspicious message.

## Quick Check

Think of a vendor or contractor you work with regularly. If they sent you a file right now claiming it was "urgent," would you open it?

## Report It

If a vendor requests unusual access, sends unexpected files, or asks you to bypass normal procedures, report it. Supply chain attacks often start with a compromised vendor account that looks legitimate.
