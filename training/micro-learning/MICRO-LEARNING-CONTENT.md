## Month 1 (Aug 2026): Phishing Awareness

**Headline:** Think Before You Click — Phishing Is Still the #1 Entry Point

**Why it matters:** 90% of data breaches start with a phishing email. Attackers impersonate trusted services like Drata, Google, or internal IT to trick you into revealing credentials or installing malware. One click can compromise Aira's entire ISMS.

**What to do:**
1. Check the sender's actual email address — hover over the name, don't trust the display name.
2. Never enter credentials after clicking a link in an unsolicited email. Navigate to the service directly.
3. Look for urgency or fear ("act now," "your account will be deleted") — these are red flags.

**Quick check:** Think of the last unexpected email you received. Did you check the sender address or just the sender name?

**Report it:** Forward suspicious emails to your IT/security contact. Reporting is a strength, not an embarrassment.

**ISO Link:** A.6.6, A.8.23 | **Date:** August 2026 | **Aira ISMS Awareness Programme**

## Month 2 (Sep 2026): Password Hygiene

**Headline:** Your Passwords Are Keys to Aira's Systems — Don't Hand Them Out

**Why it matters:** Weak or reused passwords are responsible for 80% of hacking-related breaches. When you reuse a password across services, one compromised site gives attackers keys to everything else — including Aira's Drata console, Google Cloud, and internal tools.

**What to do:**
1. Use a password manager to generate and store unique, long passwords for every service.
2. Never share passwords via email, chat, or tickets — use a secure sharing mechanism if needed.
3. Change passwords immediately if you suspect they've been exposed (use haveibeenpwned.com to check).

**Quick check:** Do you have any password you've used on more than one service? If yes, change it today.

**Report it:** If you receive a password reset email you didn't request, report it immediately — someone may be targeting your account.

**ISO Link:** A.5.17 | **Date:** September 2026 | **Aira ISMS Awareness Programme**

## Month 3 (Oct 2026): Clean Desk & Clear Screen

**Headline:** Lock It or Lose It — Physical Security Starts at Your Desk

**Why it matters:** Information is visible on screens, sticky notes, and printed documents. A visitor, cleaner, or even a passing colleague could see sensitive Aira configuration data, API keys, or client information. Clean desk habits are an ISO 27001 requirement and protect against visual eavesdropping.

**What to do:**
1. Lock your screen every time you walk away — use Windows+L or Control+Command+Q (Mac).
2. Store sensitive printed documents in locked drawers; shred them when no longer needed.
3. Remove sticky notes with passwords, access codes, or configuration details from your workspace.

**Quick check:** Look at your desk right now. Is there anything a visitor could read that they shouldn't?

**Report it:** If you notice sensitive information left exposed in a shared area, notify the owner or your security contact.

**ISO Link:** A.7.1 | **Date:** October 2026 | **Aira ISMS Awareness Programme**

## Month 4 (Nov 2026): Social Engineering

**Headline:** Hackers Don't Always Hack Computers — Sometimes They Hack People

**Why it matters:** Social engineering bypasses technical controls by manipulating people. Attackers may impersonate Aira vendors (Drata support, Google Cloud rep), new team members, or even executives. They exploit helpfulness, fear of authority, and urgency to get what they want — access tokens, configuration details, or direct system access.

**What to do:**
1. Verify unexpected requests through a second channel — call the person back on a known number, not one they provide.
2. Be especially cautious of requests to bypass normal procedures, share credentials, or install remote access software.
3. "I need to verify this with my manager" is always an acceptable answer.

**Quick check:** If someone called you claiming to be from "Google Cloud support" and asked for your API key, what would you do?

**Report it:** Report any unsolicited request for sensitive information, even if you didn't fall for it. Your report protects the next person.

**ISO Link:** A.6.6 | **Date:** November 2026 | **Aira ISMS Awareness Programme**

## Month 5 (Dec 2026): Data Classification

**Headline:** Not All Data Is Equal — Know What You're Handling

**Why it matters:** Aira handles customer data, system configurations, audit evidence, and internal communications. Misclassifying data leads to over-sharing, under-protecting, or storing sensitive data where it doesn't belong. ISO 27001 requires you to handle information according to its classification level.

**What to do:**
1. Label every document and dataset with its classification: Public, Internal, Confidential, or Restricted.
2. Share Confidential data only with people who have a business need — not "everyone on the team."
3. Never store Restricted data (credentials, API keys, PII) in shared drives, chat, or unencrypted locations.

**Quick check:** Look at the last document you shared. Did you think about who really needed access?

**Report it:** If you discover sensitive data stored in an inappropriate location (e.g., credentials in a Google Doc), report it.

**ISO Link:** A.5.12 | **Date:** December 2026 | **Aira ISMS Awareness Programme**

## Month 6 (Jan 2027): Incident Reporting

**Headline:** See Something, Say Something — Speed Saves in Security Incidents

**Why it matters:** The average time to detect a breach is over 200 days. Early reporting dramatically reduces damage. If you click a phishing link, notice unusual system behavior, or spot a misconfigured security setting, the worst thing you can do is stay silent. ISO 27001 requires timely incident reporting — and it's the single most important security action you can take.

**What to do:**
1. Report immediately, even if you're unsure — suspected incidents count. Don't wait until you're "certain."
2. Preserve evidence: don't delete emails, close windows, or restart the machine.
3. Know your reporting channel: email security@ or use the incident form — know it before you need it.

**Quick check:** Do you know exactly how to report a security incident right now, without looking it up?

**Report it:** This one is the report. If something feels off — report it. Reporting is the north star of our security programme.

**ISO Link:** A.6.8 | **Date:** January 2027 | **Aira ISMS Awareness Programme**

## Month 7 (Feb 2027): Secure Remote Work

**Headline:** Your Home Office Is Part of Aira's Security Perimeter

**Why it matters:** Remote and hybrid work extends the security boundary to coffee shops, co-working spaces, and home networks. Public Wi-Fi, shared devices, and shoulder-surfing create risks that don't exist in the office. Aira's Drata integration and Google AI access are only as secure as the environment you connect from.

**What to do:**
1. Always use the company VPN when connecting to Aira systems from outside the office.
2. Never work on sensitive Aira data from public Wi-Fi without a VPN — use your phone hotspot if needed.
3. Keep work on work devices. Don't access Aira systems from personal laptops, tablets, or shared family computers.

**Quick check:** When was the last time you connected to Aira systems from outside your home? Were you on a secure network?

**Report it:** If you suspect someone saw your screen while working in public, or if your work device is lost/stolen, report it within the hour.

**ISO Link:** A.6.7, A.8.16 | **Date:** February 2027 | **Aira ISMS Awareness Programme**

## Month 8 (Mar 2027): MFA & Authentication

**Headline:** MFA Is Your Bodyguard — Don't Approve Requests You Didn't Make

**Why it matters:** Multi-factor authentication is one of the most effective defenses against account takeover — it blocks 99.9% of automated attacks. But MFA fatigue attacks are rising: attackers flood you with push notifications, hoping you'll approve one just to make it stop. For Aira's Drata and Google Cloud access, MFA is non-negotiable.

**What to do:**
1. Never approve an MFA prompt you didn't initiate — if you get one, someone has your password.
2. Use app-based MFA (Google Authenticator, hardware token) instead of SMS where possible — SMS is interceptable.
3. If you receive repeated MFA prompts, change your password immediately AND report the incident.

**Quick check:** If your phone buzzed with an MFA approval right now while you're reading this, what would you do?

**Report it:** Unsolicited MFA prompts mean your password is compromised. Report immediately.

**ISO Link:** A.5.15 | **Date:** March 2027 | **Aira ISMS Awareness Programme**

## Month 9 (Apr 2027): Software Updates & Patching

**Headline:** Update Now, Not Later — Unpatched Software Is an Open Door

**Why it matters:** Software vendors fix vulnerabilities monthly, but attackers exploit unpatched systems within days. A single unpatched machine on Aira's network can be the entry point. This applies to your workstation, development tools, Google Cloud SDK, and any services integrated with Drata.

**What to do:**
1. Install OS and application updates within 48 hours of release — don't snooze indefinitely.
2. Restart your machine at least weekly to apply pending updates that require a reboot.
3. Never disable automatic updates — if a legitimate compatibility issue exists, report it and use the approved workaround.

**Quick check:** Check your system right now — are there pending updates waiting?

**Report it:** If an update breaks a tool you need for Aira work, report the issue. Don't disable updates to work around it.

**ISO Link:** A.8.8 | **Date:** April 2027 | **Aira ISMS Awareness Programme**

## Month 10 (May 2027): Data Disposal

**Headline:** Deleting Isn't Enough — Dispose of Data the Right Way

**Why it matters:** Deleted files, old emails, and decommissioned devices can contain years of Aira configuration data, client information, and audit records. Simply dragging to the trash or formatting a drive doesn't actually destroy the data. ISO 27001 requires secure disposal procedures — and it applies to both digital and physical media.

**What to do:**
1. Use secure deletion tools for digital files; for paper, use a cross-cut shredder rated P-4 or higher.
2. Before returning or decommissioning a company device, follow the offboarding checklist to wipe it properly.
3. Review and purge old files quarterly — don't hoard sensitive data "just in case."

**Quick check:** When was the last time you securely deleted a file containing Aira configuration data?

**Report it:** If you find old hardware (USB drives, hard drives, old phones) with company data, report it for secure disposal — don't just throw it away.

**ISO Link:** A.7.14, A.8.10 | **Date:** May 2027 | **Aira ISMS Awareness Programme**

## Month 11 (Jun 2027): Mobile Device Security

**Headline:** Your Phone Is a Portal to Aira's Systems — Protect It Like One

**Why it matters:** Phones and tablets carry email, MFA apps, chat history, cloud access tokens, and possibly Drata mobile alerts. A lost or compromised mobile device can give attackers access to Aira's monitoring infrastructure, Google Workspace, and authentication. Mobile threats (malicious apps, smishing, Bluetooth attacks) are rising fast.

**What to do:**
1. Set a strong device passcode (6+ digits or biometric) and enable auto-lock after 2 minutes or less.
2. Only install apps from official app stores — sideloaded apps from unknown sources are a major malware vector.
3. Enable "Find My Device" and remote wipe — if your phone goes missing, you need to be able to wipe it fast.

**Quick check:** Is your phone set to auto-lock? If you left it on a coffee shop table right now, how long before it locks?

**Report it:** Report a lost or stolen device within one hour. Timing matters — remote wipe before compromise is the goal.

**ISO Link:** A.8.1 | **Date:** June 2027 | **Aira ISMS Awareness Programme**

## Month 12 (Jul 2027): Supply Chain Awareness

**Headline:** Your Vendors' Security Is Your Security — The Chain Is Only as Strong as Its Weakest Link

**Why it matters:** Aira's ISMS depends on third-party services (Drata, Google AI, cloud providers, SaaS tools). If a supplier is breached, Aira's data may be exposed. ISO 27001:2022 adds dedicated controls for supply chain security (A.5.19–A.5.22). Every team member who selects, configures, or uses a third-party service bears responsibility.

**What to do:**
1. Before adopting a new tool or service, check whether it has been through Aira's vendor security review process.
2. Monitor supplier security notifications — if Drata or Google announces a breach, assess impact on Aira immediately.
3. Don't connect unsanctioned third-party apps or browser extensions to Aira's Google Workspace or cloud environment.

**Quick check:** What third-party services does your work depend on? Have any of them had a security incident in the past year?

**Report it:** If a vendor or service you use notifies you of a security incident affecting your data, report it immediately.

**ISO Link:** A.5.19 - A.5.22 | **Date:** July 2027 | **Aira ISMS Awareness Programme**

## Reinforcement Tools

### Quarterly Quiz Template (Administered every 3 months)

A 5-question quiz covering the last 3 topics. Example:

Q1: What should you check before clicking a link in an email? (Phishing — Month 1)
Q2: True/False: It's OK to reuse a strong password across two services. (Passwords — Month 2)
Q3: What should you do every time you walk away from your desk? (Clean Desk — Month 3)
Q4: Describe one red flag that indicates a social engineering attempt. (Social Engineering — Month 4)
Q5: What's the data classification level for API keys and credentials? (Data Classification — Month 5)

### Manager Talking Points (Optional, per month)

One sentence managers can drop into team stand-ups:
1. "Remember: hover over sender addresses before clicking links."
2. "Password reuse is the root cause — use the password manager."
3. "Clean desk check after stand-up — screens locked, papers away."
4. "If someone calls claiming to be support and asks for credentials, what do you do?"
5. "Check the classification on any document before sharing it."
6. "Do you know how to report an incident? Say it out loud."
7. "VPN on before connecting to Aira outside the office."
8. "Never approve an MFA prompt you didn't request."
9. "Check for pending updates — apply them by end of day."
10. "Old data isn't harmless — schedule a purge session."
11. "Phone auto-lock under two minutes — verify yours right now."
12. "New tool adoption needs security review — no exceptions."
