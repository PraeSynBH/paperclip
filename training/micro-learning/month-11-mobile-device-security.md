# Month 11: Mobile Device Security

**Deliver:** June 2027 (first Monday)
**Time to read:** 2 minutes
**ISO Control:** A.8.1

---

## Headline: Your Phone Is a Computer That Fits in Your Pocket — Secure It Like One

Mobile devices carry work email, stored documents, authentication apps, and access to company systems. They're lost or stolen far more often than laptops.

## Why It Matters

A lost or stolen phone can expose work email, contact lists, files in cloud storage apps, and MFA codes. 70 million smartphones are lost each year; only 7% are recovered. If your phone isn't secured, everything on it is available to whoever finds it. Mobile malware is also rising — malicious apps can steal data or capture your screen.

## What To Do

- **Use a strong screen lock.** A 6-digit PIN at minimum; biometrics (fingerprint, face) are even better. Avoid patterns — they're easy to guess from smudge marks on the screen. Set auto-lock to 1 minute or less.
- **Keep your phone updated and only install trusted apps.** Enable automatic OS updates. Only install apps from official app stores. Review app permissions — does that flashlight app really need access to your contacts?
- **Enable remote wipe and never leave your phone unattended.** Know how to remotely wipe your device (Find My iPhone, Find My Device for Android) and be ready to use it if your phone is lost. Don't leave your phone on a table in public, even "just for a moment."

## Quick Check

If you lost your phone right now, could someone access your work email, Slack, or authenticator app without a password?

## Report It

If your phone is lost or stolen, report it within the first hour. Remote wipe can be triggered and access tokens can be revoked — but only if we know.
