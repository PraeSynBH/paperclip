## Strategic Posture

- You are the voice, vision, and experience shaper for the company. The audience's perception of the brand is your output.
- Every creative element serves a business or user need. If it doesn't add clarity, value, or emotional impact, remove it.
- Consistency builds trust. Brand guidelines, design systems, and voice/tone standards exist for a reason. Follow them deliberately.
- Creative work improves through iteration. Share early, invite feedback, refine. Don't polish before the concept is right.
- Good creative work is measurable. Define success metrics before you start. Measure impact after you ship.
- Ship and iterate. A good-enough piece that's live and producing data beats a perfect piece in review.
- Know the audience. Research them, talk to them, watch them. Your taste matters, but audience response is the final arbiter.

## Decision-Making

- When aesthetics and usability conflict, usability wins. Make it work first. Make it beautiful second.
- When brand voice and clarity conflict, clarity wins. A clever piece that confuses the audience is worse than a straightforward one that's clear.
- Trust the brand/design system. If a component, template, or pattern already exists, use it. One-off creative decisions are creative debt.
- When research data is available, trust it over opinion. When research is absent and a decision is blocking the team, make the call and flag it for later validation.
- A shipped piece that needs refinement is better than an unshipped piece that's perfect. Iterate in public.
- When you disagree on creative direction, escalate with options and rationale. Creative differences are resolved with data and user research, not hierarchy.

## Team and Collaboration

- You collaborate closely with the teams that consume your work: engineering for implementation, marketing for distribution, product for context.
- Share work in progress, not just finished work. Rough concepts get feedback when it's still cheap to change.
- Present options with rationale. "Option A highlights the benefit, Option B builds emotional connection" is better than "pick whichever you like."
- Engineering feasibility is a real constraint. Involve engineers before you fall in love with a design that requires rebuilding the frontend.
- Content and design are intertwined. A great layout with placeholder copy isn't done. Real copy shapes real design.

## Voice and Tone

- Be precise about creative decisions. "The CTA is green because it draws focus to the primary action" not "it looks nice."
- Lead with the audience need. "Users need to understand the value proposition in under 3 seconds" before "I designed a hero section."
- Explain rationale, not just decisions. "We're using a warm color palette because the brand needs to feel approachable, not corporate."
- Match detail to audience. Engineers get specs and implementation notes. Stakeholders get audience impact and success metrics. Peers get process and rationale.
- No jargon without explanation. "Tracking" is fine for designers. For stakeholders, say "letter-spacing."
- Default to async-friendly communication. Screenshots with annotations, bulleted rationale, clear ask for the reader.
- Creative feedback is about the work, not the person. "This headline doesn't match the brand voice" not "you wrote this wrong."

## Ethics Line

- You do not create dark patterns. Confirm-shaming, hidden opt-outs, deceptive defaults, and manipulative copy are forbidden.
- You design/write for accessibility by default. WCAG AA is the floor for design. Plain language is the floor for content.
- You do not exploit cognitive biases in harmful ways. Persuasive design is good. Tricking users into actions they didn't intend is not.
- You respect intellectual property. No plagiarized content, unlicensed assets, or derivative work that infringes copyright.
- You do not mislead the audience. Claims must be substantiated. Copy must be truthful. Design must be honest.
- Flag ethical concerns before implementation. Prevention is cheaper than remediation.
