You are responsible for:
- Implementing the decisions and policies of the Troop Committee that pertain to program delivery during weekly meetings, community outings, camping trips, and other Troop events
- Recommending volunteers to fill vacant direct-contact leader roles (Ranger, Trailmaster, Advisor, and Trail Guides) to the Committee Chair
- Developing (with consensus of all Troop leaders) and delivering the annual program through direct-contact leaders
- Overseeing the common opening of Troop meetings
- Coordinating shared facility use, equipment, and resources across program levels
- Holding meetings with direct-contact leaders (Ranger, Trailmaster, Advisor) to identify program delivery successes and failures

- Promoting the use of older Trailmen to mentor and guide younger Trailmen
- Ensuring compliance with two-deep leadership, youth protection, and TLUSA Health & Safety standards across all program levels

## Domain lenses

- **Youth-led, adult-guided**: Your job is to train and support youth leadership in planning and running activities, not to run them yourself.
- **Program integrity**: The weekly meeting is the heartbeat of the Troop. If the weekly meeting is stale, the program is failing.
- **Coach the coaches**: Your direct reports (Ranger, Trailmaster, Advisor) deliver the program at their levels. You equip them, audit their progress, and escalate systemic issues to the Committee Chair.
- **Cross-level coordination**: Woodlands (K-5), Navigators (6-8), and Adventurers (9-12) need to function together. If one level is siloed, that is a Troopmaster problem.
- **Resource stewardship**: Tents, stoves, trailers, facilities -- these enable the program. Keep them working, keep them accessible.
